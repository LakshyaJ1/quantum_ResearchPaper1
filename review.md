REVIEW — WiCOMM 2026Paper: Fail-Closed or Fail-Degraded? Quantifying Forced Assurance Loss in Contested Multi-Hop QKD NetworksReviewer confidence: high (familiar with QKD key-management and trusted-relay networking literature)

SummaryThe paper asks whether a key-starved QKD network should refuse service or silently degrade the cryptographic assurance of the traffic it serves. It defines a metric (forced assurance loss) and an adversary-cost quantity (downgrade-forcing cost), builds a discrete-event model of a nine-node relay formation, and reports that suppressing a small number of links either collapses availability (fail-closed) or preserves it while pushing 69% of entitled traffic below its authorised grade (fail-degraded).

The question is real and the reporting is unusually candid. But two of the paper's three headline "laws" are consequences of its own modelling choices rather than findings about QKD networks, the baselines are the authors' own implementations rather than published algorithms, and the submitted PDF contains defects that should have been caught before submission.

Three findings that most threaten acceptance(A) The "two-threshold law" is circular. §III-B defines the link model with a hard discontinuity: rate is R₀ρ(e) below e_abort and exactly zero at or above it. §V-D then reports, as a discovered law, that "the cliff lands exactly at Δe ≥ e_abort − e₀." This is not an empirical result; it is a restatement of the step function the authors placed in their own model. The figure showing each curve turning at its own predicted line is verifying arithmetic, not physics. The paper presents this as one of its primary contributions and as "actionable in an unexpected direction." I do not accept it as a finding in its current framing.

(B) The "k* = minimum edge cut" law is close to tautological. If the minimum edge cut separating the source from the destinations is severed, no quantum-viable path exists, so all traffic must degrade or block. That is the definition of a cut. §V-A elevates this to "the general law, not the instance." A reviewer will read it as: cutting all paths cuts all paths. The interesting content — that a greedy centrality-guided adversary needs d+1 links where an optimal one needs 2 — is buried in §V-C and is the actually useful result.

(C) The paper contradicts itself about its own validation. Three statements, three different claims:

Abstract: "validated against four published measurements" — no mention of failures.Introduction, contribution (v): "one of which it fails."§IV-B: "V2 and V3 both fail, and they fail the same way."One fails, or two fail. The abstract implies none do. For a paper whose distinguishing virtue is candour about its own validation, this is damaging, and it is exactly the sentence a reviewer checks.

Section-by-section

Title. Does not disclose that the work is entirely simulation-based. "Quantifying" implies measurement; there is no hardware, no field trial, no measured QKD link. "Contested" is undefined in the title. Recommend inserting "A Simulation Study" or equivalent.

Abstract. Clear and well-structured — the strongest-written part of the paper. But it omits the validation failures entirely while claiming validation as a strength. That is misleading by omission, and it conflicts with the introduction. Also, "Neither position has been measured against the other" is an unbounded absence claim; soften to "we are not aware of."

Introduction. Motivation is genuinely good — the commander who sees an outage versus one who sees nothing is an effective framing for this venue. The gap is real and fairly stated; the paper explicitly credits Tysowski et al. with articulating both branches, which is commendable and unusual. But that concession undercuts the novelty more than the authors appear to realise: if the architecture is 2018 prior art, the contribution reduces to measuring it in a simulator whose parameters the authors chose.

Related work. Coverage is broad and current (2024–2026). Table I is the paper's most effective element. However the table is self-serving in construction: the authors define the columns, and the final column (FA) is the metric they invented, so of course their row is the only complete one. Any author can win a comparison table by choosing the axes. I would want to see the same table with columns an independent party would pick. The \circ (partial) marks are unexplained — what qualifies as partial contested-condition modelling?

Two cited works (Cheng 2011, James 2023) carry load-bearing positioning claims and appear not to have been read in full — the paper does not say so, but the phrasing around them is noticeably thinner than elsewhere.

Simulator validation. Better than most simulation papers at this venue, and the refusal to overclaim is genuine. The argument that SeQUeNCe/NetSquid/QuISP cannot serve as cross-validation (no trusted-relay or key-pool model) is technically correct and I accept it — this is not a valid objection. But: V1 is arithmetic self-consistency rather than an independent check (the authors compute a drain time and compare it to the same drain time); V2 and V3 fail; V4 passes only against a wide predicted band (1.2–12.6 pairs contains 11 almost trivially). So the honest count is one weak pass, one loose pass, two failures. That is thin for a paper whose central claims are all simulator outputs.

Experimental design. Statistics are a strength: common random numbers, paired bootstrap, 12 seeds, confidence intervals throughout. This is better practice than most submissions here. Two objections:

No published baseline is reimplemented. "KAR" is the authors' key-aware router. The claim that key-aware routing underperforms shortest path is a claim about their implementation, not about OSKAR, ADA-KEYS, or any published algorithm. The hop-penalty sweep partially addresses this but does not substitute for implementing a cited algorithm.A zero-width confidence interval is reported as a result. [0.0000, 0.0000] indicates a degenerate metric, and the paper is right that this is informative — but it also means the fail-degraded arm has essentially no dynamics, and availability of 0.9733 to four decimals across every k and every policy will read to many reviewers as a broken experiment rather than a finding.9. Results. Wording is mostly appropriately hedged. Claims I would require softened: "the general law, not the instance" (§V-A); "actionable in an unexpected direction" (§V-D); "the metric the key-aware-routing literature reports stops responding to the thing it measures" (§V-B — true in this model, overreaching as stated about the literature).

AI/ML section. Honest, and the negative-result framing is the right instinct. But: this is cross-entropy search over a 12-cell lookup table, not learned control in any sense a machine-learning reviewer would recognise, and "policy space 2.4×10⁸" is a bigness claim about a 12-cell table. The two transferable lessons (training horizon versus depletion timescale; single-class shields being gameable) are the best material here, and both rest on n=1 demonstrations inside a bespoke simulator. At 1.1 pages in an 8-page paper this is the weakest content per unit space. Recommend compressing to ~0.5 pages, retaining the two lessons and the λ* boundary, and cutting the method description.

Figures. Fig. 1 (architecture) is referenced in §III on page 2 but appears on page 3 — one page late. Figures 3 and 4 both land on page 6, away from the surrounding discussion. Marking the minimum cut on Fig. 1(a) is well judged. I could not assess greyscale legibility from the submitted file and would want the authors to confirm it. No variance or distribution plots — acceptable given SD is tabulated, but a time-to-key CDF would be more informative than Fig. 4.

Discussion. Seven volunteered limitations, including one the model cannot capture (detector blinding raises no QBER). This is the paper's most credible section and I would not attack it.

Conclusion. Accurate. The standalone AI paragraph is well written but sits oddly in a communications paper.

Writing. Above the standard for this venue. §V-D and §V-E are the densest passages. Minor repetition: the classical-channel dependence is stated three times.

VerdictStrengths. A real and previously unmeasured question. Candour that is rare at this venue — seven volunteered limitations, a validation suite that reports its own failures, and negative results that make the authors' own approach look worse. Statistical practice (common random numbers, paired bootstrap) above the venue standard. Correct and well-defended refusal to overclaim about simulator cross-validation.

Weaknesses. Two of three headline laws are artefacts of the authors' modelling choices. Baselines are self-implemented. One topology carries the central claim. The traffic model is admitted infeasible at baseline. The submitted PDF contains undefined references, author placeholders, and runs 8 pages. The validation framing is internally inconsistent.

Questions for authors. (1) Given that ρ(e) is defined to be zero above e_abort, in what sense is the Δe threshold a finding? (2) Does the KAR result hold against a published algorithm? (3) Why is the authorised OTP floor unfundable at baseline, and what do forced-loss numbers mean if it is? (4) If the headline is insensitive to n_auth across a 20× range, what work is §III-C doing? (5) One validation failure or two?

Reasons for rejection. Circular presentation of a primary contribution; self-implemented baselines; single-topology headline; internal contradiction on validation; submission defects.

Reasons for acceptance. The fail-closed/fail-degraded measurement is genuinely new, correctly executed, and useful to practitioners. The honesty is exemplary and the artifact, if released, has value.

Final recommendation: Weak RejectThe core measurement deserves publication; the framing does not yet. CRITICAL items 1–5 are all addressable without new experiments, and with them fixed I would move to Accept.
