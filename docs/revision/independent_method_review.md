# Independent methodology review

This review inspected the revised scientific code, registered learning protocol, completed main-grid records, and deterministic theory records. It did not author or change simulation code, alter results, interrupt jobs, run simulations, or inspect incomplete final-test learning data. The only created artifact is this note. Findings are ranked by the consequence of making an unsupported claim, rather than by an assumption that a large experiment must be rerun.

The central corrections are scientifically defensible. Generation failure no longer invalidates accepted stored key; timely service and floor-compliant service use the same arrival cohort; the cut inequality is a necessary resource condition; and learned actions are separated from the deterministic exception cap. No numerical implementation defect requiring a restart of the main grid was found in this bounded review. Several claim and evidence limits still require attention before camera-ready delivery.

Finding 1 — High claim risk: the current adaptive ranking is offline graph adaptation, not routing-reactive retargeting.

`revision/scenarios.py:106-121` recomputes source-to-reachable-target centrality after hypothetical edge deletions while constructing an attack order. `revision/scenarios.py:138-145` then chooses one fixed attack set before simulation. At runtime, `revision/scenarios.py:166-167` only enables or releases that set; no observed service route, inventory, or traffic event changes the targets. The attack-cost sensitivity in `experiments/revision_suite.py:85-88` does not introduce an observation/update cadence.

The separate depth check is useful but has a different scope. `experiments/revision_theory_checks.py:190-209` compares offline rankings at depths one through six and explicitly describes its scope as edge removal without inventory feedback at lines 238-242. The completed artifact gives historical static isolation count seven at depth six, canonical-name static count eight, and residual-graph/adaptive and minimum-cut counts two. This refutes a universal interpretation of the old static-ranking depth penalty. It does not establish an online attack's finite-time grade-loss cost. The method also changes the centrality endpoint population as well as recomputation timing, so it is not an isolated experiment on recomputation alone.

Required disposition: preserve the existing explicit offline qualification in `docs/revision/theory_checks.md`, withdraw the general linear-depth claim, and distinguish any new route-feedback experiment from these completed records. The coordinator has acknowledged this distinction and plans a separately identified online experiment. It must not be attributed to the current frozen main or learning code.

Finding 2 — Medium evidence gap: the main case supports a finite low-load regime, not a queue-stability proof or complete per-link accounting report.

`revision/scenarios.py:23-30` fixes a 24-hour horizon, ISR scale 0.1, initial spendable fraction 0.02, and seven authentication charges. The traffic definitions at `qkdsim/config.py:168-195` and debit formula at lines 100-113 imply a minimum floor-compliant offered debit of

`0.05(1385) + 0.002(145129) + 0.03(1257) = 397.218 bit/s`

per common route crossing. This is below the nominal 480 bit/s of two 240-bit/s branches. A feasible mean-flow argument also needs route allocation and per-edge constraints; comparing total generation over all links with one end-to-end demand would count different relay resources as interchangeable. Even a valid mean-flow certificate does not prove zero deadline loss or stochastic stability with finite stores and intermittent transport.

The completed formation baseline is informative: fail-closed ISR availability is 0.9699655 and fail-degraded ISR qualified availability is 0.9695051, averaged over the twelve seeds. These support the scoped claim that the revised 24-hour baseline does not deny or degrade most ISR traffic. They do not alone establish unlimited-duration sustainability. A read-only inventory check over all sixty no-attack, fail-closed topology/seed runs shows that preferred KAR paths can draw down while alternatives accumulate. For example, formation edge BDE1–REL1 loses a mean 0.605613 Mbit between initial and final inventory. This is consistent with the routing policy and is not by itself a defect, but rules out a claim that every store replenishes during the baseline.

`docs/revision/REVIEWER_RESPONSE.md:91` currently says per-link inventory/generation/consumption is reported. `experiments/revision_suite.py:144-149` saves per-link initial/final inventory but only aggregate generated, consumed, and discarded counters. Required disposition: correct that reporting claim, or attach a separately identified targeted per-link accounting audit. Retain the response's current finite-scenario qualification at lines 93-95. Use a route-aware fluid-feasibility calculation if the term sustainable is retained, and keep it distinct from a stability proof.

Finding 3 — Medium claim risk: the stressed initial stock strongly conditions the headline loss.

The default reservoir has 51,380,224 spendable bits at full capacity (`qkdsim/config.py:133-136`). The constructor at `revision/scenarios.py:208-213` therefore gives 1,027,604.48 initially spendable bits per edge in the main case. This is two percent of capacity above the protected reserve, not two percent of total stored bits. The initial quantum stock, concurrent cut size, demand, and attack duration jointly determine finite-horizon outcomes. The main full-cut outcome cannot be presented as a typical full-reservoir deployment or a consequence of cut cardinality alone.

The registered initial-stock sensitivity at `experiments/revision_suite.py:66-67` includes 0.02, 0.1, and 1.0, which can address this dependence when complete. Required disposition: disclose the two-percent initial state prominently, including in the interpretation of the headline; retain unfavorable or near-zero attack effects at larger stock. The current suite does not sweep reservoir capacity or the arrival horizon (`experiments/revision_suite.py:50-101`), despite exposing these parameters in Case. Do not claim those sweeps are completed. A limitations statement is sufficient if those experiments are outside the final scope.

Finding 4 — Medium claim risk: the horizon intervention does not uniquely identify depletion, and test intervals condition on selected policies.

The learning split is clean by inspection: training seeds 1000–1005, validation 2000–2003, and final test 5000–5011 (`experiments/revision_learning.py:38-44`). Restart selection accepts exactly the registered validation cells (lines 300-318). The freeze records selected tables, source/runtime identity, protocol, and test plan (lines 427-456); test requires that freeze before evaluation (lines 516-543). No test-driven tuning path was found in this implementation. This is a static design assessment; final evidence must still pass the completed-result analyzer.

Short and full nominal training differ in horizon, with the same six realization seeds and paired optimizer seeds (lines 107-115 and 384-395). Full nominal therefore receives four times the simulated arrival exposure. It also encounters different state frequencies, training-score precision, and potentially different candidate populations after the first generation. The short run performs its deadline drain without the later competing arrivals that exist in the full run. These are meaningful parts of a horizon intervention, but a performance difference is not a controlled attribution solely to reserve depletion.

The final analyzer correctly averages the seven fixed conditions within seed before resampling twelve seed units (`experiments/revision_learning_analysis.py:110-131,377-408`). These intervals quantify realization uncertainty conditional on the frozen selected controller and the fixed condition set. They do not quantify uncertainty over repeated CEM training/validation selections, nor over an unknown deployment-condition population. Two optimizer restarts followed by selection are not twelve independent training replications. Required disposition: label these intervals accordingly and describe the comparison as horizon/environment-training sensitivity. The robust objective is an equal-condition mean, not minimax training (`experiments/revision_learning.py:274-297`).

Finding 5 — Medium interpretation risk: arrival-cohort outcomes and the cut bound use different natural time sets.

`qkdsim/sim.py:582-589` stops new arrivals at the horizon but continues generation/outages until all pending requests finish or expire. The attack is active only before `attack_start_s + attack_duration_s` (`revision/scenarios.py:166-167`), so a main attack releases at 86,400 seconds while deadline drain continues. This is coherent cohort follow-up and avoids treating censoring as denial. However, a request arriving before T may complete after T and may benefit from replenishment after release.

The analytical set in `docs/revision/finite_reservoir_theory.md:69-80` counts compliant sessions delivered by time T. It must not be equated silently with every arrival before T counted in the simulator summary. Required disposition: state that main metrics follow a 24-hour arrival cohort through deadlines and that attacks last the stated interval. If a numerical cut-bound comparison is made against those complete outcomes, use the actual completion horizon and its generated key, or restrict both sides to completions by the same T. No main rerun is required merely because this distinction exists.

Finding 6 — Medium scope limits: graph cuts, runtime caps, and simulated fallback are narrower than deployment guarantees.

The weighted source/super-sink construction at `revision/scenarios.py:85-99` correctly targets all forward terminals. It is exact for the defined structural isolation cost. The budgeted prefix selection at lines 140-145 is a heuristic at sub-cut budgets and is not optimized for timely assurance loss. Fibre costs 1/10/100 are relative assumptions, not measured physical expenditure. Bounded routing uses at most four graph-static candidate paths and a shortest-plus-two-hop limit (`qkdsim/network.py:133-158`), so observed availability also depends on that route set.

The exception monitor enforces below-floor completions against all class arrivals, including blocked and pending requests (`revision/policies.py:121-159`). Its rational cap and sequential observation are consistent with the stated prefix contract; the independent completion-event audit is at `experiments/revision_learning.py:199-217`. This does not guarantee a ten-percent maximum among served requests, a maximum payload exposure fraction, or a cryptographic security property. The guarantee belongs to the monitor, not to CEM.

The separate fallback study explicitly assumes sufficient unexpired shared secret and omits finite stock, expiry, refresh, and fallback authentication costs (`experiments/revision_fallback.py:1-6,169-174`). Its KEM-service outage is a global abstract gate at lines 46-50. It can demonstrate dependence on the modeled establishment service versus transport. It cannot determine real preplaced-key lifetime or prove a deployable fallback implementation. Current guide/response wording largely states these limits correctly; carry them into the manuscript without widening the claims.

Finding 7 — Lower reproducibility risk: complete the archival metadata and preserve undefined outcomes.

The main runner writes NaN with `allow_nan=True` (`experiments/revision_suite.py:157-159`), so its files are Python-readable JSON but not strict JSON. Undefined conditional quantities are scientifically appropriate; replacing them with zero would be wrong. Prefer a documented portable export using null with raw counts while preserving original records. Main source hashing does not incorporate NumPy/NetworkX versions and its manifest records Python/OS only (lines 30-37 and 183-188). The learning fingerprint does include dependency versions (`experiments/revision_learning.py:80-93`). Attach the verified environment/lock information to the final artifact and avoid claiming source hashes alone establish byte-identical reproducibility across runtimes.

Confirmed evidence and analytical scope

A separate read-only pass parsed all 360 records named by `manifest_main.json`. All source hashes and seeds matched the manifest. In all class and aggregate rows, qualified plus degraded equaled served, and served plus blocked equaled arrivals; class arrivals summed to aggregate arrivals. Within each of the sixty topology/seed groups, all six mode/budget cells had identical arrival hashes. Maximum absolute reported global conservation residual was 1.49011611938477e-7 bits. This validates the saved accounting and pairing assertions; it is not external validation of rates or protocols. Fixed-dimension exogenous draws at `revision/scenarios.py:158-180` separately support identical environment streams for same-topology paired runs. Main records do not save an environment hash, so that latter conclusion is code-based.

The finite cut-budget derivation is valid under its stated assumptions. Summing per-edge nonnegative remaining-stock constraints yields an optimistic upper bound on cumulative compliant debit across any cut. It is necessary, not sufficient; intermediate deadlines and unpoolable edge stores explain why equality can fail. The completed deterministic records genuinely demonstrate the distinction: three branches with 1,900 bits per edge permit a pooled bound of seven but serve only six; four branches with 749 bits each permit a pooled bound of three but serve none. These facts follow from whole-request debit requirements, not from a fitted stochastic result. The main one-link-attack ring and grid results also exhibit nonzero assurance loss below the full two-edge structural cut, which is a model counterexample to a universal finite-time forcing-cost equality.

The completed statistics are marginal seed-bootstrap intervals, not simultaneous coverage or multiplicity-adjusted tests (`experiments/revision_analyze.py:173-179`). Keep the full grid available and avoid selecting only favorable contrasts. Do not promote a zero-width interval caused by identical outcomes into a certainty claim about real deployments. The manuscript remains a simulation study of a specified key-service controller with illustrative resource and outage assumptions; no new hardware measurement, cryptographic proof, or universal optimality result is established by these records.
