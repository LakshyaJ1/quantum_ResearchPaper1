Fail-Closed or Fail-Degraded? A Simulation Study of Forced Assurance
Loss in Contested Multi-Hop QKD Networks
Lakshya Jain, Siddhi Arora and Divya Mishra
The revision responds to the nine main concerns, eight separately tracked minor comments, and
four explicit questions in the oﬃcial attachment through implemented corrections, completed com-
parisons, and explicit limits where broader calibration or deployment claims are unsupported. It
replaces the original universal cut/depth claims with ﬁnite-horizon evidence, corrects the formation
to eight nodes, and preserves a substantive held-out policy-learning evaluation.
Throughout this response, service means timely key supply or modeled session admission. The
simulator does not execute payload encryption, packet delivery, or a KEM. G2 classiﬁes information-
theoretic key delivery with computational payload encryption. The main case retains valid stored
keys and begins with 2% of spendable capacity; the purge alternative removes working stock while
preserving the protected reserve.
The 0.35-second fallback setup delay, class priorities, outage
processes and attack weights are declared assumptions.
Let A be timely service per arrival, Q ﬂoor-compliant service per arrival, and L = A−Q below-ﬂoor
service per arrival. F counts G1/G2-entitled arrivals served at G3/G4, divided by all entitled ar-
rivals; FB weights that same entitled population by associated payload bits. These volume measures
are not observed compromise. Percentages below are means of seedwise proportions; diﬀerences are
percentage points (pp), except the unitless utility J = A −0.75L. Brackets give marginal 95%
percentile bootstrap intervals with 20,000 resamples. Appendix A identiﬁes each experiment’s seed
count and veriﬁcation record. No comparison pools duplicate conﬁguration aliases as independent
samples.
Page references below identify the authoritative six-page manuscript PDF exported from the ﬁnal
Word ﬁle, Paper167_Manuscript.pdf.
Section, ﬁgure and table identiﬁers refer to that version.

R1-05: Heterogeneous attack cost
Revised manuscript: Section IV-B (p. 3), Section V-C and Table III (p. 5).
The revised budget is the sum of relative weights on simultaneously suppressed links: free-space
weight one, ﬁber weights one, ten or one hundred. Fiber is unavailable to a budget-two selector
when its weight exceeds two. This is budget-conditioned exclusion, not a physical claim that ﬁber
cannot be attacked.
The weighted source/super-sink solution is exact for all-terminal structural isolation. Partial ranked
selections and ﬁnite-horizon forcing are not optimality claims. In the formation budget-two sensi-
tivity, static betweenness gives F = 58.654 [58.147, 59.314]% at each tested ﬁber ratio, whereas the
oﬄine reachable-target ranking changes from 89.824 [89.222, 90.462]% at ratio one to 10.133 [8.721,
11.517]% at ratios ten/hundred. The weighted-cut result is 89.850 [89.231, 90.507]% in these cells.
Thus method and assumed costs must both be speciﬁed.
The separate online study also reports realized relative-cost link-hours, distinct links touched and
target changes; concurrent capacity and cumulative exposure are not interchangeable. Equipment,
sensing and retargeting costs remain unmodeled.

R1-09: Scope and evidence for the policy search
Revised manuscript: Section IV-A (pp. 2-3), Section V-D and Fig. 3 (p. 5), and Section VI (pp. 5-6).
The ﬁxed-policy crossing is algebra: ∆J = ∆A −λ∆L, so λ∗= ∆A/∆L when ∆L̸ = 0. Search
produces policies and outcomes; it is not needed to derive that crossing. We withdraw the old
104.8%-of-best-search claim as evidence of global optimality or the uselessness of learning.
The completed study searches 18-state/ﬁve-action tables by categorical cross-entropy, with ten
tables, four generations, two optimizer restarts and six training rollouts per candidate. Training
seeds 1000-1005, validation 2000-2003 and ﬁnal test 5000-5011 are disjoint. Each regime’s restart
is selected on three conditions times four validation seeds; tables, code/runtime identity and the
seven-condition ﬁnal plan are frozen before test. The monitor permits zero C2 exceptions and at
most ﬂoor(0.1 times class arrivals) ISR/LOG committed exceptions at every preﬁx.
Across the ﬁxed seven-condition benchmark, multi-condition training has ∆J = 0.001066 [0.000191,
0.002032] versus budget-greedy. The six-hour nominal and 24-hour nominal regimes instead have
-0.004678 [-0.005774, -0.003459] and -0.003607 [-0.003955, -0.003394]; full nominal selects strict-ﬂoor
behavior. The multi-condition gain accompanies the payload/ISR trade-oﬀin R1-07. Every audited
cap holds.
There were 1,440 requested training rollouts, 1,110 unique executions; 72 validation requests, 60
unique; and 420 ﬁnal-test executions. Conditions receive equal weight within seed before bootstrap-
ping twelve seed averages. Intervals condition on selected tables; robust training optimizes a mean,
not a worst case. The horizon protocols also change simulated exposure and state coverage, so they
do not uniquely identify depletion as a cause.
Evidence: docs/revision/learning_protocol.md, docs/revision/LEARNING_RESULTS.md, and
results/revision/learning_analysis/analysis.json.

R1-Q3: Does retargeting preserve the apparent linear depth cost?
Revised manuscript: Section V-C (p. 5) and Section VI (pp. 5-6).
We withdraw the general linear-depth claim. The original static chain result depends on ranking
and tie conventions: historical ties give d + 1 structural removals, canonical edge names give d + 2,
and an oﬄine reachable-target ranking needs two at depths two through six. These are structural
checks, not ﬁnite-time forcing costs.
We then ran 126 online-study simulations at depths 2, 4 and 6, concurrent budgets one/two, and six
seeds. The route-feedback selector updates every 60 seconds using recently committed positive-key
routes and current transport reachability; it has no inventory contents or future information. Its
release-on-unusable rule gives two-slot excess F of 73.27 [71.03, 75.08]%, 52.82 [50.98, 54.67]% and
35.90 [33.22, 38.84]% across those depths. However, realized exposure falls from 43.89 to 38.95
link-hours, while the ﬁxed cut spends 48. Releasing targets during transport outages can allow
subsequent replenishment. Equal slot caps therefore did not mean equal persistence/exposure.
